## ⚠️ Important Note on "CISA Secure AI" in SCF 2026.2

After a thorough search of the SCF 2026.2 Knowledge Base, **"CISA Secure AI" does not appear as a named direct mapping** within SCF 2026.2's 252+ framework mappings. This is likely because:

- CISA has released several related AI guidance documents (e.g., *"Deploying AI Systems Securely"*, *"Guidelines for Secure AI System Development"* co-published with the UK's NCSC, and *"Secure by Design" AI guidance*), but these are **voluntary guidelines**, not a formal regulatory standard with numbered controls
- SCF 2026.2 primarily maps to **mandatory standards and formal frameworks** with citable control numbers
- CISA's AI security guidance largely **aligns with and references NIST AI RMF** and **NIST AI 600-1**, which **are** directly mapped in SCF

---

## ✅ What IS Mapped: The Closest Aligned Frameworks in SCF

The SCF **AAT (Artificial Intelligence & Autonomous Technologies)** domain — with **126 controls** — directly maps to the frameworks that CISA Secure AI guidance is built upon:

| Framework in SCF | Relevance to CISA Secure AI |
|---|---|
| **NIST AI 100-1 (AI RMF 1.0)** | Foundation CISA references for AI risk governance |
| **NIST AI 600-1** | Generative AI specific guidance CISA endorses |
| **ISO 42001:2023** | AI management systems standard |
| **EU AI Act** | International AI regulation CISA aligns with |

## 💡 Practical Recommendation

If you need to demonstrate compliance with **CISA Secure AI guidance** through SCF:

1. **Use NIST AI 100-1 / NIST AI 600-1 as your primary SCF mapping** — CISA explicitly endorses these, and they are formally mapped in SCF
2. **Focus your assessment on the SCF AAT domain** (126 controls) — this is the most directly relevant
3. **Target SCR-CMM Level 3** (Well Defined) as the baseline — requires formal AI governance program, GRC oversight, and dedicated AI governance team/function
