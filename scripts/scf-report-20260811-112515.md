# Personnel Security: ISO 27001:2022 vs. NIST 800-53 R5
## Mapped Through SCF HRS (Human Resources Security) Domain

These two frameworks share the same **goal** — protecting organizations from insider and personnel-related risk — but differ fundamentally in **structure, prescriptiveness, and evidence requirements**.

---

## 🏗️ Structural Difference at a Glance

| Dimension | **ISO 27001:2022** | **NIST 800-53 R5** |
|---|---|---|
| **Type** | Certifiable management system standard | Federal control catalog (broadly adopted) |
| **Style** | **Principles-based** — defines *what* to achieve | **Prescriptive** — defines *how* to implement |
| **Personnel Controls** | Annex A, Clauses 6.1–6.5 & 7.2–7.3 | **PS-01 through PS-09** (+ enhancements) |
| **Evidence Model** | Competency records, awareness evidence | Formal policies, signed artifacts, defined timelines |
| **Audit Method** | Third-party certification (e.g., UKAS, ANAB) | Assessment against SP 800-53A procedures |
| **US Federal Use** | ❌ Not designed for US gov | ✅ Mandatory for federal systems |

## 🔍 Key Differences by Topic Area

### 1. 📝 Policy & Program (HRS-01)
| | ISO 27001 | NIST 800-53 R5 |
|---|---|---|
| Requirement | Personnel must be *aware* of security responsibilities (7.3) | Formal **PS-01 policy document** with defined scope, roles, review cadence |
| Evidence | Awareness training records, competency evidence | Standalone written policy + procedures, reviewed annually |
| **Gap** | ISO allows awareness to satisfy this | NIST requires a *distinct, documented* Personnel Security Policy |

---

### 2. 🔎 Pre-Employment Screening (HRS-04)
| | ISO 27001 | NIST 800-53 R5 |
|---|---|---|
| Requirement | Background checks required (7.2(b–c)); *content* is org-defined | **PS-03**: Screening before access; tied to **position risk designation**; re-screening defined |
| Specificity | Defers to local legal context | Enhancements (PS-03(01)) require *independent third-party* screening |
| **Gap** | ISO is silent on re-screening and risk tiers | NIST requires frequency, risk-tiered criteria, and documented results |

---

### 3. 🎭 Position Risk Designation (HRS-02)
| | ISO 27001 | NIST 800-53 R5 |
|---|---|---|
| Requirement | Defines role *competencies* (7.2(a)); no formal risk tiers | **PS-02**: Every position must have a formal **risk designation** (Low/Moderate/High) |
| Integration | Risk addressed through Clause 6.1 risk assessment — not HR-specific | Dedicated PS-02 control — directly drives screening rigor |
| **Gap** | ISO achieves this implicitly through risk treatment, not HR artifacts | NIST requires a formal, documented position-risk register |

---

### 4. 📋 Terms of Employment / Rules of Behavior (HRS-05)
| | ISO 27001 | NIST 800-53 R5 |
|---|---|---|
| Requirement | Personnel must understand security obligations (7.3(a–c)) | **PL-04**: Signed **Rules of Behavior (RoB)** required *before* system access |
| Signed Document | ❌ Not explicitly required — awareness can suffice | ✅ Mandatory signed artifact; must be re-signed when updated |
| System-Specific | No | Yes — RoB is often system/role-specific |
| **Gap** | ISO allows training acknowledgment; NIST requires enforceable signed agreement |

---

### 5. 🚪 Termination & Transfer (HRS-09, HRS-08)
| | ISO 27001 | NIST 800-53 R5 |
|---|---|---|
| Termination | ISO 27002 6.5 — responsibilities after employment ends | **PS-04**: Revoke access, retrieve authenticators, exit interviews, notify parties — *with defined timeframes* |
| Transfer | ISO 27002 6.5 — change of employment responsibilities | **PS-05**: Access must be adjusted *within defined timelines*; enhancement adds automated notification |
| **Gap** | ISO leaves timelines to organizational discretion | NIST prescribes *time-bound* actions and requires documented evidence |

---

### 6. 👥 Third-Party / Contractor Personnel (HRS-10)
| | ISO 27001 | NIST 800-53 R5 |
|---|---|---|
| Approach | Addressed via **Supplier Management** (Annex A 5.19–5.22) — not HR-specific | **PS-07**: Dedicated control — security requirements in contracts, notification on departure, compliance monitoring |
| HR Focus | ❌ Treated as a vendor/procurement issue | ✅ Treated as a personnel security issue |
| **Gap** | ISO 27001 has no direct Annex A HR control for third-party workers | NIST treats contractors as first-class HR security subjects |

## 📊 Summary Scorecard

| Topic Area | ISO 27001 | NIST 800-53 R5 | Winner on Prescriptiveness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Policy documentation | Implied | Explicit PS-01 | NIST |
| Pre-employment screening | Org-defined | Risk-tiered, re-screening | NIST |
| Position risk tiers | Implicit | Formal PS-02 | NIST |
| Signed agreements | Not required | Mandatory PL-04 RoB | NIST |
| Termination timelines | Discretionary | Time-bound PS-04 | NIST |
| Contractor personnel | Supplier lens | Dedicated PS-07 | NIST |
| Certifiable | ✅ Yes | ❌ No | ISO |
| Principles flexibility | ✅ High | ❌ Low | ISO |

## 💡 Key Takeaways

1. **ISO 27001** is **outcome-oriented** — it tells you *what* state to achieve (aware, competent, screened personnel) and lets you define *how*. This makes it flexible but harder to audit consistently.

2. **NIST 800-53 R5** is **process-oriented** — it tells you *exactly* what artifacts, timelines, and procedures to implement. More burdensome, but leaves less room for interpretation.

3. **Dual compliance is achievable** — The SCF **HRS-01 through HRS-10** controls satisfy *both* frameworks simultaneously. Organizations that implement SCF HRS at **SCR-CMM Level 3 (Well Defined)** will generally satisfy both ISO 27001 Annex A and NIST 800-53 R5 PS family requirements with a single control set.

4. **If you're only doing ISO 27001**, you can satisfy personnel requirements with lighter documentation. **If NIST applies** (FedRAMP, CMMC, FISMA), expect to produce signed RoBs, formal position risk designations, and time-stamped access revocation records.
